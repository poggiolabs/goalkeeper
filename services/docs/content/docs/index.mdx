**Goalkeeper gives teams one shared record for long-running work: the goal, the
definition of done, and the progress so far.**

<GoalFlock />

Teams today are composed of people and agents. Agents are good at tasks that fit
in one run. Longer jobs are harder. The outcome gets buried in a chat, context
is lost between runs, and progress turns into a list of activity instead of a
clear answer to “is it done?”

Goalkeeper keeps the important parts outside any one agent session. A goal
records the outcome, success criteria, accountable person, timeframe, and a
history of status, health, and evaluation updates.

Goalkeeper does not run agents or tell them how to work; it gives the whole team
the same record, even when the work moves between sessions, runtimes, or agents.

## Why goals?

Several lines of research point in the same direction:

- **A shared blackboard lets specialists coordinate without talking to every
  other specialist.** The pattern goes back to the
  [Hearsay-II blackboard system](https://doi.org/10.1609/aimag.v7i3.550): each
  participant reads the current state and adds what it knows. In a 2025 study,
  [Google researchers found](https://research.google/pubs/blackboard-multi-agent-systems-for-information-discovery-in-data-science/)
  that LLM teams using a blackboard improved end-to-end task success by 13–57%
  over RAG and master–worker baselines.

- **Long jobs fail at persistence, not just capability.** In the 2026
  [PushBench preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23574), agents made plausible
  local progress but repeated work, stopped early, or lost track of the target.
  A controller that tracked verified state reached 69–78% success and eliminated
  duplicate submissions; standard and completion-gated controllers completed
  no tasks in some matched settings.

- **Separate records for the task and its progress make recovery explicit.**
  Microsoft's
  [Magentic-One](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/magentic-one-a-generalist-multi-agent-system-for-solving-complex-tasks/)
  keeps a task ledger for facts and plans and a progress ledger for status and
  assignments. Its orchestrator checks for stalls and replans, and the system
  performed competitively on three agent benchmarks.

- **Useful work compounds when it survives the run.**
  [Voyager](https://voyager.minedojo.org/) stored successful behaviors in a
  reusable skill library. It collected 3.3× more unique items and reached key
  milestones up to 15.3× faster than prior systems, then reused those skills in
  a new world. The durable artifact was more valuable than any one session.

- **A stable objective can keep a very large search pointed in one direction.**
  In Anthropic's 2026
  [Riemann zeta experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta),
  roughly 60 subagents explored, checked, reviewed, and formalized work over 31
  million output tokens. Human input was mostly encouragement to keep going.
  The system did not solve the Riemann hypothesis, but it improved a related
  lower bound from 41.6% to 67.2%.

These results motivate a narrow bet: keep the outcome, success criteria,
verified progress, and evidence in one durable place, then let agents come and
go around it.
